How this works

A thought here is one complete cycle in which a mind pulls in information from all of its parts, combines it, and settles on a result that becomes the starting point for the next cycle: one chip cycle, one pass through an AI model, a moment of human perception, a considered decision. Richer thoughts are built from many such cycles, and each one still needs every part to check in on time.

The thought radius is how far a signal travels during one thought: radius = signal speed × thought time. The calculator compares the distance between a mind's parts with that radius.

  • One mind: parts are within a tenth of the thought radius. A single thought needs many back-and-forth signals, so a tenth is a generous allowance.
  • A team: from a tenth of the radius to 10 times the radius. The parts must specialize and coordinate.
  • A society: from 10 to 10,000 times the radius. Separate minds exchanging messages.
  • A civilization: beyond 10,000 times the radius. Separate minds that share history, not thoughts.

The thresholds are judgment calls, chosen to make the pattern visible. The pattern does not depend on them: whatever lines you draw, faster thinking shrinks every zone, and nothing can make the signals faster than light.

Signal speeds: light in space 299,792 km per second; optical fiber about two-thirds of that; slower long-range nerve fibers about 10 meters per second (nerve speeds range from about 1 to 120 m/s). Mars uses its average distance from Earth; the real distance swings from about 55 to 400 million km.
